what about making this a money game


Worked for 8s
Short answer
You can make it money-related, but peer-to-peer “bet on this 1v1” is the riskiest version. For Upset City, money works better as entry / prize / status, not as a betting app.

What people usually mean by “money game”
Model	What it is	Fit for outdoor 1v1
Side bets (“$20 on this run”)	Players stake each other	High drama, high legal/fraud risk
Entry fee → prize pool	$5–25 to enter a night/bracket; winner(s) take pot	Cleaner “tournament” feel
Challenge purse	Host posts “$10 game”; accepter matches	Simple, but still wager-like
Subscription / Pro	Pay for ranked, media boosts, analytics	Safer; not really a “money game”
Sponsored pots	Brand funds ATX night; free entry	Best brand + legal posture
Why money is tempting (and real)
Makes people show up (no-shows hurt more when cash is on the line)
Makes games feel serious
Creates a reason to open the app when the map alone isn’t enough
Separates “shoot around with kids” from “I’m competing”
Why it can kill the product
Law — Skill contests vs gambling depends on state (Texas is not casual about this). Peer betting apps get messy fast (age, escrow, KYC, disputes).
Trust — Unverified scores + money = wars. Dual-confirm becomes mandatory, not nice-to-have.
No-shows get uglier — Now it’s theft-of-time and money drama.
Safety — Outdoor public courts + cash = real-world risk (especially if people think winners walk with cash).
Brand split — Half your users just want a hoop for their kid. Money can scare that half away if it’s the default.
Chargebacks / scams — Fake accounts, reverse after loss, “I never agreed.”
Your own kill-list (unverified scores, no-shows, empty states) gets worse with money unless the foundation is iron.

Smarter money shapes for this app
1. Optional “Stakes night” (not default)
Ranked 1v1 stays free. Separate mode: entry fee nights with clear rules, age gate, confirmed scores, reliability floor.
Public finder stays pure; money lives in Scene → Play → Stakes.

2. Club / season pass, not pot every game
$10–20/month Austin league: access to rated ladder, priority invites, ATX events. Money = membership, not betting.

3. Sponsored purse (best first money move)
Local brand funds “Friday $200 open 1v1” at one court. You run format + verification; you never hold peer-to-peer bets. Cleaner story, real hype.

4. “Skin in the game” without cash
Refundable reputation bond: deposit or credit that burns on late cancel / no-show, returns if you show. Money enforces reliability, not who scored 11 first.

5. Winner-take-entry only after dual confirm + both check-in
If you ever do pots: money doesn’t move until GPS/check-in + dual score confirm. No confirm → refund.

What I would not do first
In-app “bet $20 on Sean vs Cam” feed
Cash tips to players as the core loop
Anything that mixes kids/family court finder with gambling UI on the same home screen
Money before dual-confirm scores + reliability are solid
Practical sequence
Dual-confirm scores + reliability visible
Free scene that actually runs
Then either sponsored pot nights or optional entry tournaments
Only later (if ever) true peer stakes — with lawyers, KYC, escrow
Bottom line
Money can raise seriousness and show-up rate, but the version that fits Upset City is:

Free map forever · free casual 1v1 · optional paid / sponsored competition lanes with verification and reliability.

Not:

Every open game is a wager.

If you want to go further, the useful product question is:
Do you want money for seriousness (entry/prize), for no-shows (bonds), or for gambling energy (bets)?
Those are three different apps. Which one are you actually after?

is there something more private and less trackable than stripe?


Worked for 19s
Straight answer
More private than Stripe, yes. “Invisible / untrackable,” no — and you shouldn’t design for that. Anything that moves real money at scale either creates a paper trail or creates legal/fraud risk.

For Upset City, the right question is usually:
Who holds the money, and how little do you need to touch it?

Options, from least “platform” to more platform
Approach	Privacy feel	Trackable?	Fit for your app
Cash at the court	Highest	Low digital trail	Fine for small friend games; zero app liability
Peer apps (Venmo, Cash App, Zelle, Apple Cash)	Medium	Yes (banks/apps)	Good for stakes — you never hold funds
Direct charity donate (alz.org)	Medium	Yes (charity + their card)	Best for charity mode — you only record “I donated”
Crypto P2P	Feels private	Often still trackable; messy UX	Usually worse for normal hoopers
Stripe / similar	Low privacy	Highly tracked	Best if you ever hold pots/escrow
Stripe is trackable because it’s the grown-up path (identity, disputes, chargebacks, tax forms).
Peer apps are “more private from you” — you don’t see their bank — but not untrackable to banks/IRS.

What I recommend for your two modes
1. Charity (your Alzheimer’s model)
Most private for you and cleanest ethically:

App calculates: “You owe $13 to Alzheimer’s research”
Loser opens charity’s own donate page (already linked)
Optionally uploads receipt / marks complete
You never touch the money
That’s more private for Upset City than Stripe, and better brand.

2. Peer stakes (“money on the line”)
Don’t use Stripe unless you want to be a payments company.

App shows: “Loser owes winner $13”
Players settle via Venmo / Cash App / cash outside the app
App only tracks: amount, settled yes/no, reliability if they flake
You’re a scorekeeper + honor system, not a casino or bank.

What not to chase
“Untraceable” rails so people can hide stakes → looks like evading law / enabling shady pots
In-app crypto escrow without compliance → pain + scams
Fake privacy marketing (“anonymous betting”) → attracts the wrong users and heat
Privacy that is fair product design:

Don’t store full card numbers
Don’t require you to intermediate
Don’t put payout history on public Media unless they opt in
Keep stake amounts visible on the listing (you wanted that), settlement details private to the two players
Practical “most private” product setup
Charity → direct donate to Alzheimer’s Association (you never hold $)
Stakes → peer settle (Venmo/Cash App/cash); app only records the obligation
Stripe later only if you do real escrow pots, refunds, sponsored nights
Bottom line:
Yes — peer settle + charity direct donate is more private for your company than Stripe, because you’re not the money pipe.
Nothing honest is truly “untrackable,” and you don’t need that for a good product.
